I'm not a Microsoft Certified Trainer. I was, however, a delegate on a recent [Microsoft SC-200](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/security-operations-analyst/?practice-assessment-type=certification) course near where I live. SC-200 is Microsoft's course for security operations analysts: pretty much the job I started my career in over 20 years ago, and much of what I've been doing for the last few years. What goes around comes around.

What struck me on the course, as it did the last time I attended a Microsoft course, is how *difficult* it is to be a Microsoft trainer. The deal seems to be:

- Microsoft gives the trainer the course material, mostly a *very* large pack of annotated slides.
- The trainer is expected to deliver the course from those slides, without using their own material.
- The trainer may or may not have their own environment for practical demonstrations; Microsoft doesn't provide one.
- The delegates range from under-prepared beginners to experts with far more hands-on experience than the trainer.

So the trainer has to manage inflated expectations, material they may not like or fully understand themselves, and wildly different skill levels in the room.

Trainers are now also competing with delegates who have LLMs at their fingertips, useful for filling in explanations and jumping ahead, and with the excellent, extensive documentation on [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com).

For me, the result wasn't an effective course, and I learned less than I'd hoped despite the trainer's best efforts. I'm still confident I'll take and pass the exam soon. It was also a useful reminder of how challenging other people's jobs can be.
