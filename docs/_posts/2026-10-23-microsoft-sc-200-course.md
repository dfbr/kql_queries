## Spare a thought for the Microsoft Certified Trainer

I am not a Microsoft Certified Trainer. I was, however, a delegate on a recent [Microsoft SC-200](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/security-operations-analyst/?practice-assessment-type=certification) course in my local area. This is Microsoft's course for security operations analysts - pretty much the job I started my career doing 2<i>x</i> years ago and again what I've found myself doing for the last few years - what goes around comes around!

One of the things that struck me on the course is how _difficult_ it is to be a Microsoft trainer. I the same thought the last time I was on a Microsoft course as well. The deal seems to be:

- Microsoft gives the trainer the course material (mostly a _very_ large pack of annotated slides)
- The trainer is expected to deliver the course in line with the slides and without using their own material
- The trainer may or may not have their own environment in which to demonstrate or show the material practically - Microsoft do not provide this
- The course delegates range from woefully under-prepared and inexperienced to experts with far more experience than the trainer

They then have to deal with inflated expectations, material that they don't like or understand themselves and wildly different existing skill levels among the delegates.

The trainers also, nowadays, fighting against people who have LLMs at their fingertips (good for filling in the explanations and jumpin ahead of the trainer!) and the frankly excellent and extensive documentation and material available from [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com).

Sadly, for me this didn't make for an effective course and my learning was limited despite the best attempts of the trainer.

Even so, I'm confident I'll take and pass the exam at some point fairly soon. But it's a good reminder of the challenges of other people's jobs.
